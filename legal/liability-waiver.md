>
> The owner asked for a waiver that "pushes away all liability". **That is not legally achievable, and trying for it can backfire.** Under Malaysian law:
> - liability for fraud and wilful misconduct cannot be waived;
> - liability for gross negligence, and for death or personal injury caused by negligence, very probably cannot;
> - statutory consumer rights may not be contracted out of, if the Consumer Protection Act 1999 applies, and its unfair-terms provisions may strike down one-sided exclusions;
> - under the Contracts Act 1950, an agreement that absolutely stops a party from enforcing their rights through legal proceedings is generally void (arbitration agreements are an accepted exception).
>
> An overbroad waiver risks being struck down entirely. This draft is therefore as broad as is **plausibly enforceable**, with a clear carve-out (section 5). **A signed waiver does not replace any licence or registration the operator may need** (for example, under the Capital Markets and Services Act 2007). It does not stop a regulator from acting, and it does not waive rights that regulators or statute give investors. **[COUNSEL]** Review in particular section 3 (release) and section 4 (covenant). Consider whether a separate waiver adds anything beyond the Terms. A separately ticked, plain-language document can help show that the user was clearly told about the risks.
